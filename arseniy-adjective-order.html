2–3Usually two, sometimes three adjectives. More sounds unnatural.
opinionOpinion before fact: a beautiful old house. Your view comes first, objective facts follow.
OSASCOMPOur Silly Aunt Sally Can't Pick Old Movies: then the purpose, next to the noun.
The logic: the closer an adjective is to the noun, the more it is part of what the thing is. A leather bag is a type of bag; a lovely bag is just your opinion of it. Purpose words (running shoes, dining table, sports car) almost form one word with the noun, so they stand right next to it.

The fine print

Commas, and, and other details

The order is the easy part. These are the points that still catch upper-intermediate students.

1Comma or no comma?
  • Different types → no comma. a lovely old house · a big black dog · a small wooden box
  • Same type → comma. a warm, friendly welcome · a cheap, cheerful caféTest: can you put and between them? warm and friendly ✓ → comma. lovely and old ✗ → no comma.
2When we need and
  • Two colours. a black and white cat · a blue and yellow flag
  • After be, look, seem, feel… The room was small and dark. · She looked tired but happy.With three: The flat is bright, quiet and cheap.
3Small but important
  • Numbers and determiners come first. the first two beautiful days · my three old friends
  • a / an follows the first adjective, not the noun. an old Japanese car · a huge orange
  • Fixed pairs don't change. a little old lady · a nice big house (size can come first when it's really a compliment)
  • Don't stack. Two good adjectives beat four average ones: a battered leather sofa > an old brown worn big leather sofa.
