Small but important details

exacerbate is not just "to spoil". It means to make something that is already bad even worse: exacerbate the problem / tensions / pain. You can't exacerbate a holiday — but you can spoil one.
connoisseur doesn't have to be a professional. It's anyone with expert knowledge and good taste who can judge quality: a connoisseur of wine, a cheese connoisseur. Pronunciation: /ˌkɒnəˈsɜː/ — the stress is at the end.
Spelling: arduous — not arduouse. Like famous, dangerous, ubiquitous: adjectives end in -ous.
dwindle has no object: Our savings dwindled. — not We dwindled our savings. The -ing form is often an adjective: dwindling supplies.
on the verge of + noun / -ing: on the verge of tears, on the verge of giving up — not on the verge to give up.
know-how is uncountable: a lot of know-how, the know-how to do something — not a know-how or know-hows.
