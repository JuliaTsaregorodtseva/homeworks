Step 1 · Two tenses

The way things are vs. what's happening around now

Both tenses talk about the present, but they look at it differently. The present simple is for things that are generally true, repeated or fixed. The present continuous is for things in progress now or around now: unfinished, temporary or changing.

↻

Present simple

Signal words: always · usually · often · rarely · never · every day · twice a week · once a month · on Mondays
▶

Present continuous

Signal words: now · right now · at the moment · currently · today · this week / month · these days · still · more and more
Form

How to build them

Present simple

+I / you / we / they cook
he / she / it cooks
−I don't cook · she doesn't cook
?Do you cook? · Does she cook?
↩Yes, I do. · No, she doesn't.

Present continuous

+I'm cooking · you / we / they're cooking
he / she / it's cooking
−I'm not cooking · she isn't cooking
?Are you cooking? · Is she cooking?
↩Yes, I am. · No, she isn't.
Spelling. -s: cook → cooks, watch → watches, try → tries, go → goes, have → has. -ing: make → making, cut → cutting, sit → sitting, lie → lying, travel → travelling.

Watch out

I am work in finance.
I work in finance.
She don't eat meat.
She doesn't eat meat.
Where you work?
Where do you work?
What you are doing?
What are you doing?
Step 2 · Action or non-action?

Some verbs don't like the continuous

Non-action (state) verbs describe feelings, thoughts, senses and possession — not actions. They are not "happening"; they just are. So we don't usually use them in the continuous, even when we mean right now.

♥
likes & wantslike · love · hate · want · prefer · need
✱
thinkingknow · believe · think (= opinion) · remember · understand · mean
◉
senses & seemingtaste · smell · look · sound · seem
⚿
having & otherhave (= own) · belong · cost · depend · agree · matter
I'm needing a coffee.
I need a coffee.
This soup is tasting great.
This soup tastes great.
Are you knowing the chef?
Do you know the chef?
Quick test: can you say "I'm in the middle of …ing"? I'm in the middle of cooking — yes, an action. I'm in the middle of knowing — no! That's a state.
Careful

Same verb, two meanings

A few verbs can be a state (simple) or an action (continuous) — the meaning changes.

Step 3 · Same verb, different message

Change the tense, change the meaning

Often both tenses are correct — but they don't say the same thing. Tap a sentence to switch it between simple and continuous and see what changes.

Ask yourself: permanent or temporary? · official timetable or my own plan? · neutral fact or annoyed? · someone's character or how they're behaving today?
