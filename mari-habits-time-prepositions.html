Step 1 · Rules

Talking about habits

All of these describe the things we do in general, not things we do once.

1. Generally speaking · Overall · On the whole

They mean in most situations. They go at the beginning of a sentence and are always followed by a comma.

  • Generally speaking, I feel most productive early in the day.
  • Overall, I prefer to take it slow.
  • On the whole, I'm not a morning person.

2. Adverbs of frequency

They say how often we do things. Tap one to see it in a sentence.

after beI'm hardly ever busy in the morning.
before the main verbI usually find time to exercise.

3. tend to / tend not to

tend to means the person is likely to do something — it's what usually happens. It's followed by a verb in the infinitive.

  • I tend to wake up very early. · She tends to fall asleep before midnight.
  • Negative: I tend not to set an alarm. (also: I don't tend to…)
I tend to not set an alarm.
I tend not to set an alarm.
I tend to falling asleep early.
I tend to fall asleep early.
I sometimes am tired.
I am sometimes tired.
Always, I drink coffee.
Generally speaking, I drink coffee.

Step 3 · Rules

Prepositions of time

Think of it as going from big to small: in for longer periods, on for days, at for exact points.

inparts of the day, months, seasons, years, centuriesin the morning · in the afternoon · in June · in the summer · in 2019 · in the 1990s
ondays, dates and special days — also day + part of the dayon Monday · on Saturday morning · on 1 May · on my birthday · on weekdays
atclock times, night, the weekend (UK), festivalsat 6.30 · at midnight · at lunchtime · at night · at the weekend · at Christmas
—no preposition before this, next, last, every, today, tomorrow, yesterdaythis evening · next Friday · every morning · last summer

Note: at the weekend is British; Americans say on the weekend. Both are understood.

on Saturday morning
in night
at night
on next Friday

in this evening
