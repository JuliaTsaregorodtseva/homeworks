Rule A · Contrast

although, despite, nevertheless…

All five show contrast: the second idea is surprising after the first. What changes is what comes after them.

+ clausealthough · even thoughAlthough the shop made a loss, it stayed open.She kept working even though she was exhausted.even though = stronger, more emphatic
+ noun / -ing / the fact thatdespite · in spite ofDespite the crisis, they made a profit.In spite of having no money, he set up a business.despite ✗ of · in spite ✓ of
new sentenceneverthelessThe research was expensive. Nevertheless, it continued.formal · after a full stop or ; · comma after it

Typical mistakes

Despite of the risks…
Despite the risks… / In spite of the risks…
Although it was late, but they worked.
Although it was late, they worked.
Despite it was late…
Despite it being late… / Despite the fact that it was late…
It was late, nevertheless they worked.
It was late. Nevertheless, they worked.
Rule B · Purpose

in order to, so as to…

To say why someone does something, use an infinitive of purpose. in order to and so as to are a little more formal than plain to.

positivenegative
toShe works hard to make a profit.— (avoid plain not to)
in orderThey did research in order to understand the market.I wrote it down in order not to forget it.
so asHe moved so as to be nearer the office.She whispered so as not to wake the baby.
🧩 not goes before to: in order not to, so as not to.
🔁 When the subject changes, use so that + clause: I'm saving money so that my son can study abroad.
I went to the shop for buying milk.
I went to the shop to buy milk.
I went there for to see her.
I went there (in order) to see her.
She left early so as to not miss the train.
She left early so as not to miss the train.
Rule C · the + geography

the Thames, but Lake Baikal

💡 A trick: water that flows or moves (rivers, seas, oceans) takes the; a lake usually has the word Lake first and no article. Watch out: the Great Lakes (a group) and the Lake District (a region) do take the. Also the with deserts and mountain ranges: the Sahara, the Alps.

⚠️ Careful with these

  • research is uncountable: some research, a piece of research — not a research, researches.
  • do / carry out / conduct research — never make research. Research into or on something.
  • make a profit / make a loss — not do a profit, have a loss.
  • set up (verb, two words) → a set-up (noun): They set up the company in 2020.
  • branch: of a tree, of a company (our London branch) or of science / business (a branch of medicine).
  • conduct: verb /kənˈdʌkt/, noun /ˈkɒndʌkt/ (= behaviour).
