Step 1 · The rules

Why not just say said?

A reporting verb does two jobs at once: it reports the words AND tells us what the speaker was doing (offering, warning, denying…). That's why news reports are full of them.

with say“I'll pay £1 million to anyone who finds it.”
→ Max said that he would pay £1 million to anyone who found it.
with a reporting verb→ Max offered to pay £1 million to anyone who found it.

The difficult part is what comes after the verb. There are three patterns:

1verb + to + infinitiveagree · offer · refuse · promise · threatenMax offered to pay a reward.No person after these verbs: offered me to help → offered to help me.
2verb + person + to + infinitiveadvise · ask · convince · encourage · invite · persuade · remind · tell · warnShe asked Lena to show her notebook.You are getting SOMEONE ELSE to act, so the person is needed.
3verb (+ preposition) + -ingadmit · deny · recommend · regret · suggest · insist on · apologize for · accuse sb of · blame sb forSam denied leaving his post.After a preposition (on, for, of) the verb is ALWAYS -ing.

Negatives

pattern 1 & 2warned them not to leavenot goes BEFORE to: not to not leave, not don't leave.
pattern 3regretted not telling the truthnot goes before the -ing: apologized for not calling sooner.
denydenied taking itThe negative is inside deny, so no not: denied not taking means the opposite!

Two more ways to say it

that-clauseLena admitted that she had been in the room.With agree, admit, deny, promise, regret, suggest, recommend, insist you can also use (that) + clause. With suggest / recommend / insist: suggested that we (should) wait.
perfect gerundHe admitted having stolen the money.having + past participle after pattern-3 verbs: almost the same as admitted stealing, but it stresses that the action happened earlier.
passiveSam was accused of stealing it.accuse sb of → be accused of; blame sb for → be blamed for. The preposition + -ing stay.
admit toShe admitted (to) being there.admit + -ing and admit to + -ing are both correct. Never admitted to be.

Typical mistakes

She suggested me to check the CCTV.
She suggested checking the CCTV.
He insisted to pay.
He insisted on paying.
They accused him for lying.
They accused him of lying.
He denied to know her.
He denied knowing her.
She reminded me don't forget.
She reminded me not to forget.
He recommended me to try it.
He recommended trying it.
He offered me to help.
He offered to help me.

✏️ Spelling: stress on the 2nd syllable doubles the consonant: regretted, admitted. Stress on the 1st: no doubling: offered, threatened. (apologize / apologise: both spellings are fine.)
