⚠️ Careful with these

  • in THE red / in THE black, always with the. Movement: go into the red; back to normal: get out of the red.
  • rip sb off is separable. With a pronoun it goes in the middle: they ripped us off, not ripped off us. Passive: I've been / got ripped off. Noun: What a rip-off!
  • splash out ON sth · cut back ON sth · dip INTO your savings · pay THROUGH the nose FOR sth.
  • cut back on (verb) → cutbacks (noun): The company announced cutbacks.
  • live beyond your means ↔ live within your means. Means here = the money you have; it always ends in -s.
  • savvy is an adjective: a savvy shopper, money-savvy, tech-savvy. Informal but very common.
  • go on a shopping spree (not make a shopping spree).

Step 5 · Grammar

Showing contrast

Two ideas that don't seem to fit together: I didn't have any money + I went shopping. English has several ways to join them, and each one is followed by a different kind of grammar.

+ clausealthough · even thoughsubject + verbAlthough I didn't have any money, …
+ noun / -ingdespite · in spite ofnoun, -ing, or the fact that + clauseDespite the lack of money / not having any money, …
new sentenceneverthelessfull stop (or ;) + Nevertheless,I had no money. Nevertheless, I went.
two sideswhereas · whileclause + , whereas + clauseI went shopping, whereas my friend saved.

Try the linker switch

Pick two ideas, then a linker. Watch what has to change after it.

Concession or comparison?

although / despite / nevertheless: a surpriseThe second idea is unexpected after the first. Even though she earns a lot, she's always in the red.
whereas / while: a differenceTwo people or things side by side, nothing surprising. I splash out, whereas my sister saves every penny.

Examples

  • Although / Even though I didn't have any money, I went on a Black Friday shopping spree.
  • I went on a shopping spree in spite of / despite not having any money.
  • I went on a shopping spree despite the fact that I didn't have any money.
  • I went on a shopping spree despite the lack of money.
  • I didn't have any money. Nevertheless, I went on a shopping spree.
  • I went on a shopping spree, whereas / while my friend saved some money and skipped Black Friday.

Typical mistakes

Despite of the price, …
Despite the price, … / In spite of the price, …
Although I had no money, but I went.
Although I had no money, I went.
Despite I was tired, …
Despite being tired, … / Despite the fact that I was tired, …
Even I didn't need it, I bought it.
Even though I didn't need it, I bought it.
I had no money, nevertheless I went.
I had no money. Nevertheless, I went.
