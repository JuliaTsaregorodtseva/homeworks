The big picture

Before choosing a form, ask two questions: is have doing the work of a main verb, or just helping another verb? And if it's a main verb, does it describe a state or an action? These two answers decide whether you can use the continuous, contract it, or add got.

MAIN · STATE

Possession, relationships, illness

I have a large extended family.

She has a terrible cold.

Stative: no continuous. I'm having two sisters.

MAIN · ACTION

have + object = an action or experience

I'm having problems with my wi-fi.

have a bath / a drink / lunch / a party / a look / a chat / a go / fun

Dynamic: continuous is fine.

MAIN · OBLIGATION

have to

Do we really have to spend Christmas with your parents again?

Obligation from outside: rules, laws, other people. Questions and negatives use do / does / did.

MAIN · CAUSATIVE

have + object + past participle

We're going to have our house repainted.

I had my phone stolen.

A service you arrange, or something bad that happens to you.

AUXILIARY

Perfect forms + have got

How long has Anna been going out with James?

If I hadn't taken a taxi, I wouldn't have arrived in time.

Often contracted: I've, she'd, they've.

have vs have got

have (main verb)have got (have = auxiliary)
+I have a car.I've got a car.
−I don't have a car.I haven't got a car.
?Do you have a car?Have you got a car?
PastI had a car.I had got a car. Use had.
Contract?Usually not: I've a car. I've lunch at one.Yes: I've got, she's got
RegisterNeutral, any styleMore informal, typical of spoken British English
  • In a few fixed negative phrases, got can drop out: I haven't a clue. I haven't time.
  • have got to is for a specific obligation: I've got to make a quick phone call. have to works for general or repeated ones too: I have to wear a suit to work.
  • have got (to) exists only in the present. For other tenses, use have (to): I'll have to work late tomorrow. I'll have got to…

have something done: the details

Form: have (any tense) + object + past participle. Word order matters: the object goes before the participle.

TenseExample
present continuousWe're having the kitchen redecorated.
past simpleI had my hair cut on Friday.
present perfectHave you ever had your bag searched at an airport?
modal / infinitiveYou should have that mole checked. · I need to have my eyes tested.

💡 get something done means the same and is a little more informal: I got my car serviced. The agent, if you need it, comes with by: We had our photos taken by a professional.

  • I had my hair cut. = a hairdresser did it · I cut my hair. = I did it myself.
  • We had our car stolen. Nobody arranged this. It's the "bad experience" meaning.

Classic mistakes

Do you have got any siblings? → Do you have / Have you got any siblings? Pick one system; don't mix them.
I'm having a headache. → I have / I've got a headache. Illness is a state.
We had got a dacha when I was a kid. → We had a dacha… No had got for past possession.
I must to have my passport renewed. → I must have my passport renewed.
I had repaired my laptop. (= past perfect, I did it) → I had my laptop repaired. (= a technician did it)
I've to go. → I've got to go. / I have to go.

⚠️ Careful with these

  • The it is part of the idiom and never changes: have it out with sb · have (got) it in for sb · have (got) it in you · I've had it with…
  • Prepositions: have it out with sb · have it in for sb · have a go at (doing) sth · have a soft spot for · have a bone to pick with sb.
  • have sb on is almost always continuous: You're having me on!
  • I've had it with… is present perfect and means "I've had enough". Don't confuse it with I had it (past possession).
  • Only some idioms take got: She's got it in for me. · I haven't got it in me. · I've got a bone to pick with you. · He's got a sweet tooth. But: I've got a go, we'd got a laugh.
  • egg on your face is used with have or be left with, and always means you look foolish because you were wrong.
